Our Policy on Reviews and Testimonials

By AE Tax Advisors | Educational guide | Updated September 2026

Evaluation Guides / Share Your Feedback / Our Policy on Reviews and Testimonials
No reviews on this site. This site does not publish client reviews, ratings, star counts, or testimonials. It explains how to evaluate a tax advisory firm, what to ask, and how AE Tax Advisors approaches engagements.

This site does not publish client reviews, ratings, star counts, or testimonials for AE Tax Advisors. This page explains why, what the site does instead, and the conditions that would need to be met before the policy could change. The policy is a deliberate choice, not an oversight.

The Policy in Plain Terms

The site contains no reviews. It displays no ratings or star counts. It publishes no testimonials, quotes from clients, or client stories. It does not display dollar savings attributed to clients. It does not use structured data that marks up reviews or ratings. Anything on this site that describes a situation involving a person or a business is labeled as a hypothetical or is a general explanation.

Why the Site Takes This Approach

Several reasons support the policy.

Verification. A site controlled by the firm cannot show that reviews are authentic in a way that visitors can independently confirm. Even carefully collected reviews are difficult to verify, and selected reviews can create a misleading impression.

Outcomes depend on facts. Tax outcomes depend on the taxpayer's facts, the law in effect, and documentation. A testimonial about one person's result says little about what another person should expect, and it can create unrealistic expectations. This is especially true of savings claims.

Confidentiality. Tax information is sensitive. Clients should not be pressured to disclose details about their finances, and firms have obligations to protect client information.

Regulatory and professional considerations. Rules governing advertising by tax professionals and general consumer protection rules place limits on testimonials and endorsements, particularly those that are misleading, incentivized, or not disclosed as such. Avoiding testimonials avoids those issues.

Trust. A firm that asks you to trust its own curated reviews is asking you to trust its selection. A site that instead teaches you how to check for yourself asks less of your trust and gives you more tools.

What the Site Does Instead

Instead of publishing reviews, the site provides:

  • Guides on how to evaluate any tax advisory firm, including credentials, process, and communication.
  • Checklists of questions you can ask any firm.
  • A description of how AE Tax Advisors approaches engagements, so that you can compare the approach with others.
  • A candid explanation of what the firm does not promise.
  • Guides on fees, scope, red flags, and safeguards.
  • FAQ pages that answer common questions.

What About Reviews on Other Sites

Independent platforms may host reviews of AE Tax Advisors or of any firm. This site does not control those reviews, does not endorse them, and does not verify them. If you read them, read them critically. See How to Read Online Reviews of Any Tax Firm Critically.

How to Give Feedback

If you have feedback for the firm, there is a page explaining how to send it directly. Feedback sent that way is not published. See Share Your Feedback with AE Tax Advisors.

What Would Need to Change

The policy is not permanent in principle. If the firm were to consider publishing client feedback in the future, several conditions should be met. Feedback would need to come from real clients who gave informed, voluntary permission, without incentive or pressure. It would need to be reproduced accurately and not edited to change its meaning. It would need to be presented with context, such as the date and the nature of the engagement. It would need to avoid savings claims and rating markup that cannot be verified. It would need to comply with applicable professional and advertising rules. It would need to include a clear statement that past experiences do not predict future results. Absent those conditions, the site will continue to publish none.

What You Will Not Find Here

You will not find star ratings, average scores, review counts, quotes attributed to named or unnamed clients, or claims of client satisfaction percentages. If you see something on this site that looks like a review or a rating, it is an error, and the firm would want to know about it.

A Note on Credibility

A site that promises not to show you reviews may feel unusual. But it is consistent with the aim of this site: to help you evaluate a firm on evidence that you can check yourself. The best evidence is what you can verify: credentials, written scope, clear answers to your questions, and your own impression from a conversation.

Questions You Can Ask Instead of Reading Reviews

If you would normally look at reviews, consider asking the firm questions that get at the same concerns directly. For communication, ask about response times and points of contact. For reliability, ask how the firm tracks deadlines. For expertise, ask about experience with situations like yours. For fees, ask for an all-in estimate. For problems, ask how the firm handles errors and complaints. Direct answers to these questions often tell you more than a star rating can, and you can compare the answers across firms.

A Commitment on Claims

This site also avoids claims about the size of client savings, the number of clients served, or years of experience, because such claims are difficult for readers to verify. If you want to know about the firm's background, ask directly and verify what you can.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does this mean AE Tax Advisors has no clients who are satisfied?

No. It means this site does not publish reviews. Nothing here should be read as a statement about client satisfaction either way.

Where can I find reviews?

Independent platforms host reviews of many firms. Read them critically and verify important claims yourself.
