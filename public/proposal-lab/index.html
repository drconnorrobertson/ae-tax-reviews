Focused implementation guides

Resolve the related evidence question before carrying a planning assumption into implementation.

  • Tax planning proposal deliverable boundaries: A buyer receives a broad promise to reduce taxes but no list of completed work products.
  • Tax advisory recurring fee comparison: A monthly package lists access to advisers without defining recurring tasks.
  • Tax projection assumption transparency: A proposal presents projected savings before the buyer provides complete financial information.
  • Cost segregation quote methodology: Two study providers offer different prices and descriptions of technical work.
  • Cost segregation audit support terms: A provider advertises audit support without specifying the work included.
  • Amended return engagement scope: A buyer asks for a lookback review covering several years and entities.
  • Form 3115 coordination scope: A study proposal mentions catch-up depreciation but does not identify the professional handling the tax procedure.
  • Bookkeeping cleanup opening balances: A buyer requests cleanup but the provider starts from unverified account balances.
  • Payroll correction vendor responsibility: A client needs historical payroll changes and several professionals are involved.
  • Retirement planning provider roles: A proposal combines tax planning with discussion of retirement contributions.
  • Multi-entity advisory quote coverage: A buyer owns several operating and property entities but the quote states one advisory fee.
  • State tax work exclusions: A package describes federal tax strategy but the buyer operates across state lines.
  • Implementation responsibility comparison: Two advisory proposals include recommendations but assign follow-through differently.
  • Tax adviser response standards: A buyer values responsiveness but proposals use broad terms such as priority support.
  • Tax professional credential verification: A buyer sees credentials and testimonials on a proposal or website.
  • Tax advisory refund and cancellation terms: A buyer considers an engagement with staged fees and work already begun.
  • Tax report ownership and portability: A buyer wants to change firms without losing essential history and work products.
  • Tax planning and tax preparation separation: A buyer wants advice and annual filing from one provider.
  • Tax adviser deadline readiness: A buyer requests work close to a filing or transaction date.
  • Tax notice support package comparison: A buyer expects ongoing support for notices after a return is filed.
