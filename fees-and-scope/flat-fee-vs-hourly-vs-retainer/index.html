Flat Fee, Hourly, or Retainer: Which Suits You?

By AE Tax Advisors | Educational guide | Updated September 2026

Once you understand the pricing models, the next question is which one suits your situation. There is no universal answer. Each model has strengths and weaknesses, and the best choice depends on the predictability of your needs, your preferences for cost certainty, and how much ongoing support you want. This guide compares them with practical guidance.

When a Flat Fee Tends to Fit

A flat fee often fits well-defined projects with a clear beginning and end. Examples include analyzing a possible entity change, preparing an accounting method change, designing a retirement plan, or amending a specific return. A flat fee gives you cost certainty and encourages the advisor to work efficiently. The trade-off is that the scope must be described carefully. If the project grows beyond the description, the advisor may propose a change, so ask how that process works.

When Hourly Billing Tends to Fit

Hourly billing often fits open-ended or uncertain work, such as responding to a complex notice, supporting an examination, or exploring an unusual question. It can also fit occasional questions from a client who does not need regular support. The trade-off is that the total cost is uncertain. If you choose hourly billing, ask for a not-to-exceed estimate or for notice when spending reaches certain thresholds.

When a Retainer Tends to Fit

A retainer often fits clients who want ongoing planning and access. It can support year-round conversations, projections, and check-ins, since the fee is not tied to each interaction. Some clients find this encourages them to ask questions early. The trade-off is that you pay regularly whether or not you use the service. Ask what the retainer includes and how often you are expected to meet.

Mixing Models

Many engagements mix models sensibly. For instance, a flat fee for annual preparation, a retainer for planning, and hourly billing for one-off projects can each match the nature of the work. Ask how the parts fit together and where the boundaries are. A clear diagram, even a simple list, prevents disputes.

Incentives to Consider

Every model creates incentives. Hourly billing rewards time spent, which might encourage inefficiency, though reputable firms manage this carefully. Flat fees reward efficiency, which might encourage cutting corners if scope is unclear. Retainers reward retention, which might encourage keeping clients regardless of value. None of these incentives is decisive, but knowing them helps you ask the right questions. For example, with a flat fee, ask how quality is maintained. With hourly billing, ask how time is controlled. With a retainer, ask how value is demonstrated.

A Hypothetical Comparison

Imagine three hypothetical proposals for a planning project. The first is a flat fee that covers analysis and a written recommendation. The second is hourly with an estimated range. The third is a monthly retainer with quarterly meetings. If your need is a single decision, the flat fee may be the simplest. If your situation is uncertain and may require exploration, the hourly proposal may fit. If you want year-round support, the retainer may fit. Comparing them requires converting each to a total for your expected needs. The example is hypothetical, and it does not involve any particular firm.

Clarify Payment Terms

Regardless of model, clarify when payments are due, what happens if a payment is late, and whether any part of a fee is refundable if you end the engagement early. Ask for these terms in writing.

Revisit the Arrangement

Your needs change. A project-based arrangement can become a retainer, or the reverse. Review the arrangement each year, and ask whether it still fits your situation. Do not hesitate to ask for a change if you are paying for services you do not use.

Questions to Ask About Any Model

Whatever model is proposed, ask three questions. First, what happens if the work takes longer than expected? Second, what happens if I need less than the proposal assumes? Third, how will I know before costs rise? The answers will tell you how the firm manages the relationship between scope and price, and they are more revealing than the model itself.

Consider Seasonality

Fees and availability can vary with the tax calendar. Ask whether the firm has busy periods when responses are slower or when rush fees apply, and plan your projects accordingly. Scheduling a planning project outside the busiest weeks may give you more attention and more scheduling flexibility.

Signs That the Model Fits You

You can tell that a model fits when you feel comfortable asking questions, when the bills match what you expected, and when you are using the services you pay for. If you hesitate to call because you fear the meter is running, an hourly arrangement may be discouraging you from asking useful questions. If you rarely use what a retainer includes, a project-based arrangement may fit better. Paying attention to these signals during the first year helps you adjust.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I switch models mid-engagement?

Often yes if both sides agree. Confirm the change in writing.

Does a retainer guarantee a certain amount of advice?

It depends on the agreement. Ask what the retainer includes and how it is used.
