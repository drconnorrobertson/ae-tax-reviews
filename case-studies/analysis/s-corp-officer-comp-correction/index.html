This guide explains how to evaluate the published AE Tax Advisors case about Professional Services (Consulting). It is commentary on the public case, not a new testimonial or an independent audit.

The result reported by the publisher

The source dataset lists $31,000 in reported annual tax savings. Its context field states: Officer comp ratio corrected from 3.4% to 7.3% of revenue. Read the full original case for the source narrative and assumptions.

Why the fact pattern matters

The source describes a compensation ratio changing from 3.4% to 7.3% of revenue. These ratios are facts reported about the case, not IRS-approved salary safe harbors. Copying either percentage into another owner’s payroll would skip the reasonable-compensation analysis.

The planning question to bring to AE

Evaluate actual duties, time, experience and comparable compensation rather than setting wages from a case-study ratio.

Ask the advisor to identify the return lines, documents and implementation steps that would support a comparable analysis for you. A relevant conversation should explain which facts are similar, which are different and which facts are still unknown.

How to evaluate the reported savings

Request the before-and-after baseline, the period measured and the implementation status. Determine whether professional fees, payroll costs, study costs and plan administration were included. Ask which benefits recur and which depend on a purchase, contribution or timing choice.

Do not divide a headline by an advisory fee and treat the result as your expected return on investment. The source was selected by the publisher, and the underlying returns are not available for independent verification on this site.

What a useful proposal should show

  • The objective and the client facts driving the recommendation.
  • The baseline and alternative tax calculations for the appropriate year.
  • Eligibility requirements, documentation and unresolved assumptions.
  • Who implements each step and what work is separately priced.
  • The expected timing of the benefit and any later-year consequences.

Use this case as a discussion guide

Bring the original source link to your consultation alongside current records. Ask for a written explanation rather than a promise that the reported outcome will repeat. Review the case-study methodology and engagement fee guide before choosing scope.
