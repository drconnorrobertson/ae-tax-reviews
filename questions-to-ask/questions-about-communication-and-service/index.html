Questions About Communication and Service

By AE Tax Advisors | Educational guide | Updated September 2026

Service quality is hard to judge before you hire, but you can learn a lot by asking specific questions about how a firm communicates. This guide lists questions and explains what to listen for. Clear answers suggest that the firm has thought about how to serve clients.

Who Is My Point of Contact?

Ask who will be your main contact and what happens if that person is unavailable. Some firms assign a single person, while others use a team. Neither approach is better in itself, but you should know how it works. Ask whether you will have access to the person who is actually doing the analysis, and how questions are routed.

How Quickly Do You Respond?

Ask for a typical response time to email or calls and what happens for urgent matters. Beware of vague promises such as we respond right away. A clear answer might be that routine questions are answered within one or two business days and urgent matters are escalated. Also ask how response times change during busy seasons.

How Often Will We Meet?

Ask about the meeting schedule. For planning engagements, ask how many meetings are typical in a year and when they occur. For preparation, ask when you will get drafts and how review works. If meetings are ad hoc, ask how to request one. A firm that offers a regular cadence, such as a mid-year check-in and a year-end planning meeting, shows that planning is part of its process. See Ongoing Support and Year-Round Planning.

How Will You Explain Things?

Ask how the firm presents recommendations. Do you receive written summaries? Are calculations shown? Are risks explained? Ask for an example of a typical deliverable, with client information removed. Advisors who can explain their reasoning in plain language help you make better decisions.

What Channels Do You Use?

Ask which channels the firm uses: phone, video, email, secure portal, or messaging. Ask which channels are appropriate for sensitive documents. Confirm that the firm does not ask you to send sensitive information through unsecured channels. See Questions About Data Security and Privacy.

How Do You Handle Deadlines?

Ask how the firm tracks deadlines for returns, estimated payments, elections, and extensions. Ask how the firm will remind you of things you need to do. A good answer describes a calendar and a process for reminders.

What Do You Expect From Me?

Communication runs both ways. Ask what the firm needs from you, and by when. If you are slow to provide documents, that can delay work. Understanding the expectations helps you meet them.

How Do You Handle Busy Seasons?

Tax professionals are busiest before major deadlines. Ask how the firm manages capacity. Some firms limit the number of clients, and others hire seasonal help. Ask how you will be served during the busiest weeks, and whether your work will be handled by the same team.

How Do You Handle Mistakes?

Ask what happens if the firm makes an error. A good answer describes acknowledging the error, correcting it, and explaining what changed. Be cautious of firms that suggest errors never happen. Also ask whether the firm carries professional liability insurance, which is common for professionals.

How Can I Give Feedback?

Ask how the firm collects and responds to feedback and what you can do if you are unhappy. A firm that welcomes feedback is more likely to correct problems. See Share Your Feedback with AE Tax Advisors.

What If I Want to Leave?

Ask how the firm handles the end of an engagement, including the return of records and cooperation with a new advisor. A professional transition is a sign of a professional firm. See When to Consider Changing Tax Advisors.

Setting Expectations Together

Many service problems come from mismatched expectations, not poor intent. Before you begin, agree on a simple communication plan: how you prefer to be contacted, how quickly you expect replies, how often you would like a status update, and what should be escalated. Write it down in a short email, and revisit it after the first busy season. This small step gives both sides something concrete to refer to if service feels off. It also helps the advisor learn your preferences, such as whether you want detailed explanations or short summaries.

Small Signals Worth Noticing

Notice the little things during the sales process: Did the firm show up on time? Did it send the materials it promised? Did it answer your questions or redirect them? These signals are imperfect, but they often reflect how the firm operates after you sign.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a fast response always better?

Responsiveness matters, but accuracy and clarity matter too. Look for a firm that is both timely and thorough.

Should I expect the senior professional to answer every question?

Not necessarily. Ask how the firm routes questions and when a senior person is involved.
