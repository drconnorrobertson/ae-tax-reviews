From Recommendations to Implementation

By AE Tax Advisors | Educational guide | Updated September 2026

A recommendation is only useful if it can be carried out. This page describes, in general terms, how AE Tax Advisors approaches presenting recommendations and helping clients implement the decisions they make. It is a general description, and the details of any engagement depend on its scope.

Presenting Options

After analysis, the firm typically presents options, not a single instruction. Each option is described with the facts it relies on, the expected effect on taxes and cash flow, the costs, the risks, and the conditions for it to work. You may see comparisons among alternatives, including the option of doing nothing. The goal is to help you make an informed decision.

The Role of the Client

You decide which options to pursue. The firm does not make decisions for you. It may explain which option it considers most suitable on the facts, and why, but the choice is yours. If you want more information or a second opinion, that is reasonable.

Turning Decisions into Steps

Once you decide, the next step is to translate the decision into concrete tasks with owners and dates. For example, an entity election might involve preparing a form, confirming eligibility, setting up payroll, and updating bank and accounting records. A retirement plan might involve selecting a provider, adopting a plan document, and setting up payroll deferrals. A depreciation strategy might involve commissioning a study and preparing an accounting method change. A clear task list with responsibilities reduces the chance that something is missed.

Coordination

Implementation often involves other professionals. A payroll provider may need to be told about owner compensation and benefits. An attorney may need to draft or review documents. A plan administrator may need to prepare plan documents. A bookkeeper may need to change account structures. The firm's approach is to coordinate with them, with your permission, and to communicate what needs to happen. Where a matter requires legal advice, you should consult an attorney, since the firm does not provide legal advice.

Documentation

Recommendations are supported by documentation, and implementation includes creating it. That might mean a compensation memo, a time log template, a mileage log, minutes adopting a plan, or a written lease between related parties. The firm can explain what to create, but you are responsible for maintaining records as events occur. Documentation created at the time is more persuasive than reconstructions later.

Deadlines

Many recommendations have deadlines. An election may be due by a specific date, a plan may need to be adopted by year end, or a purchase may need to be placed in service before a deadline. The approach is to identify deadlines early and to build them into the task list. If a deadline cannot be met, the firm explains the alternatives.

Tracking and Follow-Through

Implementation is not finished when a form is filed. Follow-through includes confirming acceptance, checking that payroll reflects a change, confirming that a plan was funded, and updating records. A short checklist at the end of each implementation step helps verify that it worked.

When Facts Change

If facts change during implementation, such as a delayed purchase or a change in income, the plan may need to be adjusted. Tell the firm promptly. It is better to adjust than to continue with a plan built on outdated assumptions.

What Implementation Help Does Not Include

The scope of implementation help depends on the engagement. Some engagements include coordination and preparation of forms, and others include only recommendations. The scope should be described in writing. Tasks outside the scope, such as legal drafting or investment selection, are handled by other professionals. See What an Engagement Letter Should Cover.

Measuring Progress

You can measure progress by whether the steps were completed on time, whether documentation is in place, and whether the plan is working as expected. Outcomes depend on facts and law, so progress is not the same as a guaranteed result. Regular check-ins help ensure that the plan stays on track. See Ongoing Support and Year-Round Planning.

A Short Example of a Task List

Suppose a client decides to adopt a retirement plan for the current year. A task list might include choosing a plan type and provider, confirming eligibility and employee coverage, adopting the plan document, setting up payroll deferrals, funding contributions by the applicable deadline, and recording the contributions in the books. Each task has an owner and a date. The firm might prepare the analysis and coordinate with the plan provider, while the client signs documents and approves payroll changes. Such a list turns an idea into a series of steps that can be checked off.

Confirming the Result

After implementation, confirm that the change appears where it should: on payroll reports, in the general ledger, on account statements, and later on the tax return. Small mismatches are easier to fix early than at filing time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will the firm implement everything for me?

It depends on scope. Some engagements include implementation help. Others focus on analysis and recommendations.

What if I decide not to follow a recommendation?

That is your choice. The firm can explain the consequences and help you consider alternatives.
